How a Water Heater Leak Happens

A household tank holds dozens of gallons, and all of it can reach the floor if a seam rusts through. Other leaks start small. A loose supply connector, a weeping drain valve or a temperature and pressure relief valve that keeps discharging can run for days before anyone looks. Those slow leaks soak the bottom plate of a wall long before a puddle shows.

Heaters usually sit in a garage, a hall closet, a utility room or an attic. Each location sends water somewhere different. A garage slab lets it spread out and run toward the house. A closet pushes it into drywall and the neighboring carpet. An attic heater drips through the ceiling below, and that ceiling can hold water until it sags.

First-hour safety steps for a leaking water heater

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 from outside, then call SoCalGas, the gas utility the city lists. Do not flip switches or light anything while you are inside.

For an electric heater, turn off its breaker before you step into wet areas. If the floor is wet around the panel, stay out and call 911. Close the cold water inlet valve on top of the tank, and keep hands away from the hot relief valve pipe. Then call a plumber for the repair and this line for the water.

Do not try to drain a full tank into the house to "get ahead of it." A hose run to a driveway or drain is fine once the power or gas is off. Move rugs, boxes and cardboard off the floor, because stored cardboard soaks up water and holds it against the wall.

Our Cleanup Process

  1. Confirm the source is stopped. The crew checks that the inlet is closed and the heater is off.
  2. Extract standing water. Portable pumps and extraction units pull water from hard floors, carpet and pad.
  3. Map the moisture. A moisture meter and a thermal camera show wet drywall, baseboard and slab edges you cannot see.
  4. Open and protect. Baseboards may come off so the wall cavity can dry. Wet pad is often removed, and furniture is lifted or blocked.
  5. Dry with equipment. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference materials.
  6. Clean and document. Hard surfaces are cleaned, antimicrobial is applied where the situation calls for it, and photos and readings are kept for your insurer.

Drying continues as emergency water removal hands off to monitoring visits. If the leak started at a washer or dishwasher instead, the appliance leak page covers that case.

What Changes the Cost

  • How long it leaked. A slow leak that soaked a wall is a bigger job than a puddle caught at once.
  • Where the heater sits. A garage slab dries differently from a closet with carpet next to it.
  • Flooring and wall materials. Carpet, laminate, tile and drywall each need their own handling.
  • Attic or ceiling involvement. Water above a ceiling adds access work and drying time.
  • Equipment and monitoring days. More wet area means more air movers, dehumidifiers and visits.

Scope is explained before work begins, and the plumber and heater replacement are separate from our work.

Insurance Fit

A tank that fails suddenly is the kind of sudden and accidental event many homeowners policies cover. Water that seeped for months from a corroded fitting is often treated as gradual and may be excluded, and flood is usually a separate policy. Photograph the heater and the wet areas, and keep every receipt. Check your policy and ask your insurer, because we cannot say what any policy will pay.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving the heater powered on after the tank has emptied, which can burn out an electric element or fire a gas burner on an empty tank.
  • Waiting for the puddle to dry on its own while the wall behind it stays wet.
  • Throwing away the failed tank before photos, since your insurer may ask to see the failure.
  • Opening a ceiling that holds water without letting a crew release it safely.

Duarte local note: bracing, permits and who to call

California state code requires residential water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion. When your plumber swaps a leaking unit, look at the straps before you sign off, because a heater that shifts can tear its own supply lines and start a second leak.

The city FAQ says a plumbing permit applies to water heater replacement, while also describing minor replacements as typically needing no plan submittal. Ask the Community Development counter at City Hall what applies to your swap. After a gas scare is over, LA County Fire Station 44 at 1105 Highland Ave has a non-emergency line at (626) 358-3118. Use 911 for anything active.
