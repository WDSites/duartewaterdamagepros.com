Renters Insurance and Water Damage in Duarte, CA

When a pipe bursts in a rental, two different parties are responsible for two different things. Here is who usually owns what, and what to do in the first hours.

By the Duarte Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

☎ (626) 879-0276

Renters insurance and water damage go together in Duarte, CA more than many people realize. According to Census QuickFacts, about 61.0 percent of Duarte homes are owner-occupied, which leaves roughly 39 percent of households renting. For those tenants, a leak raises an awkward question right away. Who pays for what?

The short version is that your renters policy usually protects your belongings and the landlord's policy protects the building. The details depend on your lease, your policy and the cause of the water. We are a restoration company, not a lawyer or insurer, so treat this as a way to ask better questions, not as legal advice.

Who owns what

In most rentals, the split follows ownership. You own your furniture, clothes, electronics, bedding and similar personal property. The landlord owns the walls, flooring, ceiling, cabinets, fixtures, plumbing and appliances that came with the unit. Damage to each is normally handled through the policy of whoever owns it.

That last row is where disagreements happen. Who pays for drying and repairs is a lease question and a policy question, and the answer can differ from one building to the next. Do not assume, and do not let anyone tell you it is simple until they have read the lease.

Sudden versus gradual, and flood

Renters policies usually work like homeowners policies on the question of cause. Sudden and accidental events, such as a supply line failure upstairs, are the ones more often covered. Gradual seepage and long-ignored leaks are often excluded. Flood is generally excluded under standard renters policies too, which matters if water enters from outside during a storm. Ask your insurer which category your situation falls into and what your deductible is.

If the water came from a neighbor's unit, the cause still matters. Your insurer may cover your belongings regardless of whose pipe failed, while the building repairs go through the landlord's side. Ask your insurer directly.

What to do in the first hours

  1. Stop the water if you safely can. Turn off the fixture valve. If you cannot find a shutoff, call the landlord or building manager right away.
  2. Tell the landlord in writing. A call is fine for urgency, but follow it with a text or email that states the date, the time, what you saw and where. This creates a record.
  3. Photograph and video everything. Capture the source, the wet areas and every damaged belonging before you move it.
  4. Move belongings out of the wet area. Lift items off wet floors and bag soft goods if they are clean-water wet.
  5. Keep receipts. Fan rentals, laundry, replacements and hotel stays all belong in one folder.
  6. Call your insurer. Report the loss promptly and ask what they need from you.

Who calls the restoration company

In a rental, the landlord usually controls the building, so the landlord or property manager normally decides who dries it. If the landlord is slow to respond and the water is still spreading, you can ask what they want done and keep a record of the request. Our emergency water removal crews can begin extraction once the person with authority over the unit agrees.

Ask for a written scope from whoever hires the crew. It should list the rooms, materials removed and the drying goal. Ask whether the company follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration.

Duarte local note: repairs and permits belong to the owner

After drying, the unit often needs drywall, paint or floor replacement. According to the City of Duarte FAQ, a building permit is required for drywall replacement, and a plumbing permit is required to repair or alter plumbing or replace a water heater. Those permits are the property owner's responsibility, not yours.

As a tenant, your practical step is to ask your landlord whether permits are being handled for the repair, and to keep that question in writing. If you are unsure what applies, the Community Development counter at City Hall, 1600 Huntington Drive, can be reached at (626) 357-7931 ext. 230 or 235. City Hall is open Monday to Thursday and closed Fridays, so plan your call accordingly.

Mold, health and habitability

Moisture left in a rental can lead to mold, and mold needs moisture to grow. Report any musty smell or visible growth to the landlord in writing. Questions about health effects belong with a physician. Our mold remediation page explains what remediation involves, and any job should follow ANSI/IICRC S520 practice.

For questions about your rights as a tenant, ask your landlord, your insurer, or a local tenant resource. We do not give legal advice and will not guess at your lease.
