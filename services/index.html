A water loss moves through stages, and each stage has its own work, equipment and cost. Knowing the order helps you ask the right questions when you call. The shortest version is stop the water, dry the structure, clean what was contaminated, then rebuild. Professional water work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and mold work follows ANSI/IICRC S520. Both are public references you can ask any company about.

What to do before you call

Stop the source if you can reach the valve safely. If water touches outlets, appliances or the panel, do not walk through it. Call 911 or the utility first and stay out. Sewage or storm runoff should be treated as contaminated, so keep children and pets away.

Start With the Stage You Are In

If water is still arriving or standing in a room, begin with emergency water removal. Pumps and extraction units remove the bulk water quickly, and the crew checks moisture to see where it traveled. Once the water is out, water mitigation takes over with moisture mapping, air movers and dehumidifiers.

If the water came from outside, such as storm runoff or mud from a hillside, see flood damage restoration. If materials are soaked or contaminated, water damage cleanup covers removal, sanitizing and odor control. When everything is dry, water damage repair rebuilds drywall, ceilings, insulation, floors and paint. And if mold has started, mold remediation deals with the moisture and the growth.
