Where Duarte Water Actually Shows Up Below Grade

Most Duarte homes do not have a full basement, so the cases we see are different from the Midwest picture. They include below-grade rooms and garages, hillside lots with a lower level cut into the slope, stairwells that drop below the yard, window wells, and rooms where a sump pump or sewer line is involved. If your home has any space below the surrounding ground, water can collect there.

Water arrives from a few directions. A supply pipe or water heater can fail inside. Rain can run against a wall and seep through cracks. A drain or sewer line can back up into a low floor drain.

The source decides who fixes it, so say what you saw when you call. We do not waterproof walls or foundations. We remove water, dry what it soaked and hand the source off.

Burn Scars, Debris and the Foothill Record

Duarte sits below a canyon that burned in the June 2016 Fish Fire. A USGS report records a debris flow on January 20, 2017 in a small canyon above the Las Lomas debris basin.

LA County Public Works says small mud and debris flows are possible in burn areas with less than two years of recovery, and it advises sandbags to direct runoff. If your lot is near a canyon mouth, plan before storms, not during them.

Mud and debris are a different problem from clean water. Outside flow is flood, and it is handled on our flood damage restoration page. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood and mudslide, so a separate flood policy is the usual route.

First-hour safety steps

Do not walk into a flooded basement. Water near outlets, a panel or a sump pump cord can be electrified, and deep water hides steps and objects. Shut off water at the main if a pipe is the cause. Cut power only if the panel is dry and out of the water.

Call 911 for electrical danger, gas smell or rising water that threatens people. Call a plumber for a broken pipe or the county sewer line for a backup. Then call this 24/7 line to start extraction.

Our Process

  1. Hazard check. The crew checks for electrical risk and water depth before bringing in equipment.
  2. Extraction. Submersible pumps and extraction units remove standing water from slab floors, carpet and low pockets.
  3. Moisture mapping. A moisture meter and thermal camera trace water into wall bases, framing and stored materials.
  4. Removal. Wet carpet, pad, insulation and drywall at the base of walls are cut out and bagged when they cannot be dried. Antimicrobial treatment is used on surfaces that stay.
  5. Drying. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings meet a dry standard, following the published ANSI/IICRC S500 reference.
  6. Handoff. Cleanup moves toward water mitigation steps and a plumber or contractor handles the source.

What Changes the Cost

  • Depth and volume. Deeper water needs more pumping time.
  • Water category. Sewage or outside flow requires protective gear and disposal.
  • Stored contents. Boxes and furniture have to be moved, sorted and sometimes discarded.
  • Finished versus bare space. Carpet, drywall and framing add removal and drying work.
  • Source. If water keeps arriving, drying cannot hold until it is stopped.

Insurance Fit

Sudden and accidental water from a broken pipe or failed appliance is often covered. Gradual seepage is often excluded, and flood is usually a separate policy. A National Flood Insurance Program policy typically takes about 30 days to become active, so it will not help a storm that is already on the way. Check your policy, ask your insurer and keep receipts. We do not promise approval or payout.

Prevention

Keep window wells and area drains clear of leaves and debris. Test sump pumps before the rainy season. Store valuables on shelves instead of the floor. Know where the main water shutoff is and make sure everyone in the home can reach it.
