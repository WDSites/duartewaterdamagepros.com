The Rainy Season Looks Different in Glendora

The Colby Fire began on January 16, 2014, burned 1,992 acres, destroyed five homes and forced 3,600 people to evacuate at its peak, according to the city's draft Safety Element. The burn left slopes above Glendora unable to absorb heavy rain. News reports describe debris flows in November and December 2014, with about four feet of mud against a house on Easley Canyon Road and mud on Sierra Madre Avenue, Glendora Avenue and North Rainbow Avenue.

Years later, the lesson stays. In a foothill city, the wet season is the time to check roof flashing, clear gutters and know where your shutoff valve is. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood and mudflow, so read yours before the first storm and not after.

What to Do First When Water Is Spreading in Glendora

Whatever the source, the first steps are the same, and they matter most in the first hour.

Shut it off

Close the fixture valve or the main. A leak between the street and your meter belongs to the City of Glendora Water Division.

Power first

If water reaches the panel or outlets, call 911 or the utility before anything else and stay out.

Lift and photograph

Raise belongings, then take photos of the damage before anything is moved or thrown away.

Sewage warning

If a drain is backing up, call the LA County sewer line at 1-800-675-4357 and tell our dispatcher too.

Glendora's Water, Sewer and Permit Offices

Water

The City Water Division runs about 224 miles of mains and about 13,000 meters, with bills every eight weeks. A jump between bills can point to a hidden leak.

Sewer

Sewers are primarily maintained through the LA County Consolidated Sewer Maintenance District with the city. Its 24-hour backup line is 1-800-675-4357.

Permits

The Building and Safety Division at 116 E. Foothill Boulevard handles permits through the Civic Access portal.

Emergency response

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, every day.

Sewage backups are the call homeowners most often misdirect, because the city runs the water and the county runs most of the sewer. If you smell sewage or see water rising in a floor drain, stop using the plumbing and call the county line first, then call us once water has reached the house.

Channels, Basins and Dams Above the City

Three concrete-lined channels cross Glendora, Little Dalton Wash, Big Dalton Wash and San Dimas Wash, and the city's draft Safety Element also labels the Little Dalton, Big Dalton and Morgan debris basins. Big Dalton Dam, completed in 1929, and San Dimas Dam, completed in 1922, are owned by the LA County Flood Control District, and the document treats failure as unlikely.

The same document puts built areas in FEMA Flood Zone X. That affects flood insurance pricing and rules, and it does not stop a debris flow or a plumbing failure from reaching a home. Check your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your insurer what applies. The flood damage restoration page explains the cleanup side.
