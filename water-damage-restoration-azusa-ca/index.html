Know Your Utility Before You Call

In Azusa the first question is often who supplies the water. Azusa Light & Water, the city-owned utility, provides electricity and water to all customers inside Azusa city limits and to parts of Covina, Glendora, Irwindale and West Covina. Because the service area spills beyond the city line, confirm which utility reads the meter at your address before reporting a main break.

City Public Works handles sewer and storm drain maintenance through its Streets Division. If sewage is backing up or a storm drain is clogged, report it to the city, and call us once water is inside. Water that is already in your home is our job whatever its source.

When Water Is Spreading in Azusa

Most urgent Azusa calls start with a failed supply line, a water heater, an appliance or a storm. Dispatch will ask where the water started and how far it has traveled, then tell you what to do while the crew is on the way.

Close the valve

Shut off the fixture valve or the main at the house. Opening a faucet at the lowest point drains pressure from the line.

Keep power away from water

If the panel is in a wet area you cannot reach on dry ground, call 911 or the utility before anything else.

Move belongings

Lift furniture legs onto blocks, raise rugs and electronics, and photograph everything before it is moved.

Call and describe

Give dispatch the address, the water source and whether anything could be contaminated.

Foothill Storms and Burn Areas

Azusa has had direct experience with wildfire and runoff. The Colby Fire of 2014 led to mandatory evacuations of roughly 1,000 homes in Glendora and Azusa below the burn area. The Dam Fire in July 2020 started in San Gabriel Canyon near Morris Dam, and a January 2021 storm closed State Route 39 with a mudslide and put the Ranch 2 burn scar above the Mountain Cove community on watch.

The June 2022 Fish Fire burned about 39 acres in Fish and Opal canyons within Azusa and Duarte, and LA County reported no residential impacts in Azusa. If you live near a canyon, ask the county about sandbags and check your policy, because standard homeowners insurance typically excludes flood and mudflow. The price of water damage work depends on water category, wet square footage, materials, drying days and rebuild scope.
