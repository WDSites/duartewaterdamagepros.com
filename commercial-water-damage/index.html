Commercial Water Damage Restoration in Duarte, CA

Commercial water damage restoration covers extraction, drying and cleanup for a business space. Calls are answered 24/7, and a crew is dispatched when one is free.

Commercial water damage restoration in Duarte, CA is the extraction, drying and cleanup of a business property after a pipe break, leak, overflow or storm intrusion. Dispatch takes calls 24/7 and sends the next available crew. We do not promise an arrival time.

This page is written for owners, tenants and property managers. Whoever calls, tell dispatch who controls the space, what has gotten wet and any access constraints we need to work around.

What a Commercial Job Involves

The core work is the same as in a home. Standing water is extracted, materials are checked with moisture meters and a thermal camera, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry what stays. The difference is scope. Commercial spaces often have larger floor areas, shared walls and ceilings, and several parties who need to know what happened.

We keep to what we actually do, which is the call, a scope of work, extraction, drying, cleanup and documentation. Repairs after drying, such as rebuilding or repainting, are separate work.

First-hour safety steps

Shut the water off at the fixture, the building valve or the main. Keep people and customers out of the wet area. If water is near outlets, panels, equipment or lighting, do not enter it. Call 911 or the electric utility if you are unsure.

If the source is a pipe inside the building, call a plumber to repair it. If the water is coming from the street side of the meter, call the water utility. Then call this line for extraction and drying.

Our Process

  1. Phone triage. Dispatch asks what happened, who controls the space and which areas are wet.
  2. Site check. The crew finds the source if it is still running and checks how far water traveled.
  3. Moisture mapping. Meters and a thermal camera mark the wet areas, including ceilings and wall cavities.
  4. Extraction. Extraction units and pumps remove standing water from floors and carpet.
  5. Dry and dehumidify. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until the readings reach the dry standard from the ANSI/IICRC S500 industry reference.
  6. Clean and treat. Surfaces are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial product, and HEPA vacuums handle fine dust.
  7. Document. Photos, moisture readings and a written scope are kept so the loss can be explained to an insurer, a landlord or an owner.

The drying details are explained on the structural drying page, and the overall sequence is on the water mitigation page.

Owners, Tenants and Property Managers

Responsibility depends on the lease and the cause. An owner usually controls the building and its systems, while a tenant often controls its own contents and improvements. A property manager coordinates between them. Read the lease, tell the right party quickly and ask who authorizes work. Do not wait to reduce the damage.

What Changes the Cost

  • Floor area and wet materials. More area means more equipment and more labor.
  • Water category. Contaminated water adds protective gear and disposal.
  • Construction. Concrete slab, wood framing, suspended ceilings and carpet tile dry differently.
  • Access. Locked areas, stock and furniture move setup time up or down.
  • Duration. Equipment days and monitoring depend on how fast readings fall.

Tell dispatch your access constraints so the scope reflects them.

Insurance

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered under a commercial policy, while gradual leaks are often excluded. The California Department of Insurance notes that homeowners and commercial policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Check your policy, ask your insurer or broker and keep photos and receipts. We cannot promise approval or a payout.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting for an owner meeting before shutting off the water.
  • Throwing away damaged stock or finishes before photographing them.
  • Leaving wet ceiling tiles in place because they look fine.
  • Hiring anyone who cannot show a business license and a contractor license number when it is required.

Duarte local note: checking credentials

The City of Duarte says every person or company conducting business in the city needs a business license. Use that as a reason to ask any contractor for its credentials before you sign, and apply the same standard to us.

The LA County Department of Consumer and Business Affairs says a contractor contract must show the CSLB license number, and a down payment may not exceed $1,000 or 10 percent, whichever is less. Look up the license on the CSLB website. Duarte uses ZIP codes 91010 and 91008, so give dispatch the exact address.

Sources: city of Duarte website, LA County DCBA, CSLB, ZIP directory.
