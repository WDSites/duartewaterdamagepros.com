An Honest Note About Freezing Here

Pipe-bursting freezes are uncommon in Duarte. The foothill location means some nights run colder than the valley floor, but most winter cold in this area is a frost, not a hard freeze. If your pipe damage came from a different cause, a split supply line is far more common, and the burst pipe water damage page covers that case.

Still, frozen pipe damage does happen in the real situations below.

  • A cold clear night in the foothills that drops lower than forecast.
  • Hose bibs and exposed outdoor pipes that were never covered.
  • Pipes in attics, garages and exterior walls with little insulation.
  • Vacant or unheated buildings and homes left empty during a trip.
  • Thaw leaks, where a crack opens quietly and only leaks once the ice melts.

The last one catches people off guard. A pipe can split while frozen and show nothing until it warms, and then it leaks inside a wall or ceiling with nobody home.

First-hour safety steps

Shut off the main water valve before anything thaws, then open a faucet at the lowest point. Never use a torch or open flame on a frozen pipe. Keep electrical items away from wet areas and switch off breakers only if the panel is dry and reachable.

Call 911 for gas smell, sparks or any shock risk. Call a plumber for the pipe and this line for the water. Stay out from under any ceiling that is sagging or dripping.

How We Restore a Thaw Leak

  1. Locate the wet area. A moisture meter and a thermal camera show where water traveled, including inside wall cavities and attic insulation.
  2. Extract. Pumps and extraction units remove standing water from floors, carpet and pad.
  3. Remove what will not dry. Saturated insulation and drywall are cut out and bagged. Ceiling material that holds water is released in a controlled way.
  4. Dry. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while readings are logged. The drying stage is explained under water mitigation.
  5. Treat. Surfaces are cleaned and an antimicrobial is used where the water category calls for it.
  6. Confirm. Readings are repeated until materials reach a normal moisture level, and a HEPA vacuum is used when demolition raises dust.

The work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which you can ask any company about.

What Changes the Cost

  • Where the pipe is. Attic and exterior wall leaks reach different materials than a garage hose bib.
  • How long it leaked. A vacant property can leak for days before anyone notices.
  • Insulation and drywall. Wet insulation usually has to come out.
  • Ceilings below. Water that travels downward adds rooms to the job.
  • Access. Attics and tight crawl areas change equipment and labor.
  • Drying time. Monitoring decides how long equipment stays.

Insurance Fit

A pipe that cracks and floods a room is often treated as sudden and accidental, while damage that built up slowly or came from neglect is often excluded. Policies differ on vacancy and on whether you took protective steps. Check your policy and ask your insurer. We cannot promise approval or a payout.

Prevention for Cold Nights

Cover outdoor faucets, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls and let a tap drip when a hard freeze is forecast. If you leave for a trip, know where the main valve is and who can check the home. These steps cost little and do not need a contractor.

Duarte local note: frost is not freeze

A January 2024 National Weather Service advisory for the San Gabriel Valley forecast lows of 33 to 35 degrees and advised protecting tender plants and pets. The advice to wrap or drain outdoor pipes or let them drip came only with a hard freeze warning for colder areas. Read the wording of each forecast before you decide how much to protect.

If a pipe does crack, report water damage to your insurer promptly. The California insurance regulator says to report any water damage claim immediately. EPA and CDC guidance says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours, which is why a thaw leak found late should be called in the same day.

Sources: National Weather Service, CDI claims guide, EPA, CDC.
