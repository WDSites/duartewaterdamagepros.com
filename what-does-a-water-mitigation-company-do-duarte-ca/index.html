Water Mitigation Company in Duarte, CA: What the Crew Does

Mitigation is the drying phase that decides whether a leak stays a small repair or becomes a rebuild. Here is the process, the equipment and the paperwork, step by step.

By the Duarte Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

☎ (626) 879-0276

A water mitigation company in Duarte, CA has one job in the first days after a leak, flood or burst pipe. It stops the damage from spreading. Wet drywall, subfloor and framing begin to break down quickly, and mold needs only moisture and time. Mitigation is the work that removes that moisture before it settles into the structure.

People often use three words as if they meant the same thing. They do not. Mitigation is extraction and drying. Restoration is putting finishes back, such as drywall, paint, baseboard, flooring and cabinets. Repair fixes the cause, such as a failed supply line, a cracked drain or a leaking water heater. Our water mitigation service covers the first phase, and the other two follow once the building is dry.

The process from first call to final reading

Assessment. The crew first confirms the source is stopped. Then they walk the home with a pin or pinless moisture meter and a thermal camera. The meter gives a number for each material. The camera shows cool, damp areas behind paint and tile that your eyes cannot see. Together they show where water has traveled, which is often well past the stain.

Moisture mapping. Readings are recorded room by room and wall by wall, usually on a sketch of the floor plan. This map is the baseline. Every later visit is compared against it, and it is the first document an adjuster or a skeptical homeowner will want to see.

Extraction. Standing water comes out first, using submersible pumps and truck-mounted or portable extraction units. Wet carpet and pad are lifted or cut back as needed. Removing liquid water with extraction is far faster than trying to evaporate it, so this step sets the length of the whole job.

Structural drying. Air movers are placed to push dry air across wet surfaces. Low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air so it does not just move to the next room. Small holes or cut-outs may be made in wall bases so air can reach the cavity. Count and placement come from the room size and the readings.

Monitoring visits. A technician returns, usually daily, to re-read the meters, adjust equipment and record progress. Equipment comes off only when materials reach their dry standard. A job that is declared dry without readings is a guess.

Documentation. Photos, the moisture map, daily logs and equipment records are kept for the file. This is what lets you show your insurer what was found and what was done.

Where the water came from matters

Crews classify water by contamination. A broken supply line starts out clean, a washing machine overflow is a little worse, and sewage or storm water is heavily contaminated. That last kind means removing porous materials, not drying them. Clean water also does not stay clean. Left in carpet and drywall for a day or two, it picks up bacteria from the materials themselves. That is why speed changes the method.

The standard to ask about

The published industry standard for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, the Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration. You do not need to read it. You can simply ask any company you are considering whether it follows S500, and what that means for your job in plain terms. A good answer mentions water categories, drying goals and documented readings. A vague answer is useful information too.

Also ask for a written scope before work starts. California requires a contractor license for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, and the license number should appear on your contract. You can check any license on the state contractors board website. Our crews are licensed and insured, and we are happy to talk through the scope by phone.

What mitigation is not

Mitigation is not a rebuild. Once the building reads dry, the crew may leave you with open wall bays, removed baseboard or missing flooring. That is normal. The next step is water damage repair, which replaces what was removed. Some companies do both under one contract, and others hand you off. Ask which it is before you sign.

Mitigation is also not cleaning alone. Wiping a floor and running a fan does not dry a wall cavity. If you have already done the cleanup yourself, a technician can still take readings and tell you whether anything is holding moisture.
