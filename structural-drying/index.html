Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Duarte, CA

Structural drying removes moisture trapped inside walls, floors and framing so the building reaches a measured dry standard. Dispatch is answered 24/7.

☎ (626) 879-0276

Structural drying and dehumidification in Duarte, CA is the controlled removal of moisture from building materials with air movers, dehumidifiers and measured readings. Dispatch is staffed 24/7 and assigns a crew as they become available. The goal is a dry reading on the framing, subfloor and wall cavities, not a surface that merely feels dry.

This page covers the drying science. For the whole sequence from first call to finish, see water mitigation. For pulling standing water out first, see emergency water removal.

Why Wet Buildings Need Controlled Drying

Wood, drywall, insulation and concrete soak up water and let it back out slowly. A floor that looks dry can still hold moisture in the subfloor. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and the CDC gives the same window for cleaning and drying or removing wet items.

Open windows and a household fan rarely meet that window. Moist air needs somewhere to go, and in a closed house the humidity just rises. Controlled drying moves air across wet surfaces and pulls moisture out of the air so evaporation keeps going.

Safety before equipment starts

Shut off the water source at the fixture or main valve. If water reached outlets, switches or the panel, leave those circuits off and call 911 or the electric utility if you are unsure.

Do not run household fans or space heaters over wet carpet or near wet outlets. Our crew sets up equipment on safe circuits and tells you which outlets to leave alone.

How We Dry a Structure

  1. Map the moisture. A moisture meter and thermal camera show how far water traveled, including behind baseboards and under flooring.
  2. Set a dry standard. The crew reads the same material in an unaffected area of your home and uses that as the target.
  3. Remove what cannot dry. Saturated insulation and pad, or damaged drywall, may be cut out so air can reach framing.
  4. Place air movers. Air movers aim airflow at wet surfaces, and the count depends on wet square footage and layout.
  5. Add dehumidification. LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air so the walls can keep releasing water.
  6. Dry the cavities. Small holes or lifted baseboards let warm, dry air move through walls, and mats or panels pull water from floors.
  7. Monitor and log. Crews return, record readings and move equipment until the numbers match the dry standard.

LGR and Desiccant Dehumidifiers

LGR refrigerant dehumidifiers cool air so water condenses and drains out. They suit most home jobs where the air is warm. Desiccant dehumidifiers use a drying wheel instead and can work in cooler spaces or tightly sealed areas with very wet materials. Which one fits depends on temperature, humidity and how much material is wet.

Equipment choice is not a guess. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard organizes drying around water category and class, so the plan reflects how much water, what kind and which materials are involved.

Drying Logs and the Finish Line

A drying log is the record of readings from each visit, with the dry standard beside them. It shows whether the wet areas are trending down and when equipment can come out. We stop when moisture meter readings on framing, subfloor and drywall match the dry standard, not when the room feels comfortable.

If a reading will not come down, the crew looks for the reason. A hidden leak, trapped wet insulation or a closed-off cavity is often the cause.

What Changes the Cost

  • Wet area and material. Hardwood, plaster, insulation and concrete dry at different speeds.
  • Water category and class. More contaminated or deeper water means a more involved plan.
  • Layout. Closed cabinets, double walls and crawl spaces change how many pieces of equipment are placed.
  • Duration. Equipment days and monitoring visits depend on how fast readings fall.
  • Access. Furniture, flooring and drilled openings determine setup time.

Ask for the scope in writing before the work begins.

Insurance

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks are often excluded. Flood is usually a separate policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and keep the drying log, photos and receipts. We cannot promise approval or a payout.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Turning off the equipment early because the noise is annoying.
  • Painting or sealing walls before readings are dry.
  • Moving air movers or unplugging dehumidifiers between visits.
  • Trusting a hand on the wall instead of a moisture meter.

Duarte local note: permits and the weekend

If drying shows that drywall or framing has to be replaced, the City of Duarte may require a building permit. City Hall is open Monday to Thursday and closed on Fridays, so a permit question that comes up on a Friday evening waits until Monday.

Ask the Community Development counter at City Hall, 1600 Huntington Drive, before replacement work begins. Drying itself can start right away.
