How to Fix Water Damage in the First 48 Hours in Duarte, CA

What you do in the first two days decides how much of your home can be saved. Follow this order, and know which jobs to leave to a crew.

By the Duarte Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

☎ (626) 879-0276

If you need to know how to fix water damage in Duarte, CA, the first 48 hours matter more than anything that comes after. Water that is dealt with quickly means drying. Water that sits means tear-out. EPA and CDC guidance is to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and that window is your deadline.

This sequence works for any room and any source, from a burst supply line to a failed dishwasher hose. Read the safety box first, because the most dangerous mistakes happen in the first ten minutes.

Safety first, before you touch anything

Electricity. Do not step into standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are in it. If you can reach the breaker panel without wading, switch off power to the wet area. If you cannot, call 911 or your electric utility.

Gas. If you smell gas, leave the house, do not flip switches, and call 911 from outside.

Sewage. Water that came from a toilet, floor drain or sewer backup is contaminated. Keep children and pets out, wear rubber boots and gloves, and do not try to dry it yourself.

The first 48 hours, in order

  1. Stop the source. Turn off the fixture valve under the sink or toilet if it is within reach. For a pipe break, go straight to the main shutoff, usually at the street side of the house or near the water heater. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Check power and gas. Apply the safety box above. If any doubt exists about electricity near water, treat the area as live.
  3. Photograph everything before you move it. Take wide shots of each room, then close shots of damaged walls, floors, furniture and the source itself. Video works well too. Your insurer will want these, and they cannot be retaken once things are dry.
  4. Lift valuables out of the water. Move electronics, papers, photos, rugs and small furniture to dry ground. Put foil or plastic under furniture legs on wet carpet so wood and finish do not stain.
  5. Remove standing water you can safely reach. Use a wet-dry vacuum or mop for shallow clean water. Anything deeper than a couple of inches, or any amount of sewage, is for a crew with extraction units.
  6. Ventilate. Open windows if the air outside is drier than inside. Run fans for air movement, and run the air conditioner if the house is warm. Do not use the central heating system to dry a wet area, since it can spread contamination.
  7. Take out what cannot be dried. Soaked carpet pad, ceiling tiles and wet insulation hold water for days. Bag them if safe to do so, but keep a sample or photo for the adjuster.
  8. Call for professional help. Phone a restoration crew as early as possible, ideally the same day. Hidden moisture behind baseboards and under flooring is the usual reason a "dry-looking" room develops mold later.

What to leave to the pros

Professional drying follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. You do not need to read it, but you can ask any company how it applies to your room.

Some parts of this job look easy and are not. Finding moisture inside a wall needs a meter and a thermal camera. Drying a cavity needs air movers and LGR dehumidifiers sized to the space. Anything involving sewage, ceiling drywall sagging with water, or electrical outlets in wet walls should be handled by licensed, insured people. What not to do

A few well-meant moves make things worse. Do not use a household vacuum on water. Do not stand under a ceiling that is bulging with water, because it can fail without warning. Do not switch on wet ceiling fans or lights.

Do not throw away damaged items before photographing them. Do not start repairs before your insurer has seen the damage, unless the work protects the property from further harm.

Keep receipts for anything you buy or rent to stop the damage. The California Department of Insurance notes that insurers reimburse reasonable costs to protect property, so a fan rental or a tarp is worth documenting. A written note of the date and time you first noticed the problem helps too.

When the damage has spread past one room

Water travels. A supply line leak in an upstairs bathroom often ends up in the ceiling below, and a slab leak can show up two rooms away from the actual pipe. If the stain is bigger than a dinner plate, or the room smells musty after 24 hours, bring in a crew rather than waiting.
