Choosing a Mold Removal Company in Duarte, CA

California does not license mold removers as such, so the questions you ask matter. Use this checklist before you sign anything.

By the Duarte Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

☎ (626) 879-0276

Choosing a mold removal company in Duarte, CA is harder than it should be, because the state does not set the rules for who can call themselves a mold specialist. California has no legal requirement for training, licensing or certification of mold assessors or remediators, according to CDPH. That puts the work of screening on you.

The good news is that a handful of questions sorts careful companies from careless ones fast. Use the checklist below with any company in the San Gabriel Valley, including us.

Start with the standard

Since there is no state mold license, ask the first and most useful question. What standard do you follow? A solid company will name ANSI/IICRC S520, the published Standard for Professional Mold Remediation. It is a published industry standard, not a license, and naming it does not prove quality. But a company that cannot name any standard is telling you something.

For the water side of a job, the companion standard is ANSI/IICRC S500. Both are public references, and you can ask any company how it applies them on a job like yours.

Print the table and take notes during each call. Companies are often more careful once they realize you are working from a list.

Licensing, insurance and the contract

Mold removal that includes demolition and rebuild is construction work. California requires a contractor license for jobs of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, as the CSLB sets out. The contract must show the CSLB license number, and you can check that number on the CSLB website. Look at the license status, the name on it, and whether it matches the company on your paperwork.

Insurance matters too. Ask for proof of general liability coverage and, if workers are on site, workers' compensation. Our crews are licensed and insured, and you are entitled to ask any company to show you.

Written scope and containment

A written scope should say which rooms, which materials come out, how the area is sealed off, how it is cleaned and how it is checked. A vague scope like "remove mold" leaves too much room for dispute.

Containment is worth asking about directly. Plastic sheeting seals the work area, and a HEPA filtered air machine keeps air moving inward so dust does not spread to clean rooms. If a company plans to demolish a moldy wall with no barrier, move on.

Post-work verification and independent testing

Every company should have a way of confirming that the work is done. At minimum that means moisture readings showing the structure is dry, a visual check that surfaces are clean, and a walkthrough with you before rebuild.

Some homeowners also want independent clearance testing. This is an optional, separate step, and it is best done by a different company than the one that did the removal. The reason is simple.

The company doing the work should not be the one grading it. There is no state mold exposure limit, per CDPH, so a test result is only useful when it is compared against a clear plan and a clear baseline. Ask the tester what they compare against before you pay.

Ask about the crew and the cleanup

Ask who will actually be in your home. Some companies sell the job and hand it to a subcontractor, which is not wrong in itself, but the license and insurance on the contract should cover whoever does the work. Ask how debris is bagged and carried out, how HEPA vacuums are used on the last pass, and whether the crew will walk the finished area with you before they pack up.

Red flags to walk away from

  • A price quoted before anyone has seen the damage.
  • Pressure to sign today.
  • No written scope or no license number.
  • A large down payment. LA County DCBA guidance says not to pay more than $1,000 or 10 percent, whichever is less.
  • Promises that mold will never return. No honest company can say that, because mold needs moisture and the moisture source has to be controlled.
  • Health claims or diagnoses. Health questions belong with your physician.

If the problem began with a leak, the first step is usually drying, which falls under water mitigation. Mold work that follows is covered on our mold remediation page. Ask each company how drying and remediation will be coordinated so nobody leaves the structure wet.

Duarte local note: license rules and the standard question

Because California does not license mold remediators as such, the CSLB license and a named standard are your two best filters. The LA County DCBA guide adds that the contract must show the CSLB license number and that mold cleanup must follow EPA and Cal/OSHA rules. Ask the company how it meets both.

For a quick check, write down the license number from the contract and look it up on the CSLB website before you pay anything. If the name or status does not match, call the company back. If you are unsure about a permit for demolition, the City of Duarte Community Development counter can tell you what applies at (626) 357-7931.
