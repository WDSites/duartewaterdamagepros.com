AC leak water damage repair in Duarte, CA means drying the ceilings, walls, insulation and floors that wet when an air conditioner drips or overflows. Dispatch answers 24/7, and a crew is assigned when one is free. An HVAC technician fixes the system. We dry what got wet.

Drying follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, an industry reference you can ask any company about. It is not a claim that any company holds a certificate.

Why Air Conditioners Leak

Cooling air removes moisture, and that moisture leaves as water. It drips into a pan and runs out through a condensate drain line. When the line clogs with algae and sludge, the pan fills and spills. Furnace closets and attic air handlers are the usual places, so the water goes straight into the ceiling below.

Other causes show up too. A dirty filter can starve the coil of airflow until it ices over, and then the ice melts all at once into a pan that cannot hold it. A cracked pan, a loose drain fitting or a poorly sloped line leaks the same way. Ductless wall units drip behind the wall or down the wall when their drain tube kinks or fills.

First-hour safety steps for an AC leak

Switch the system off at the thermostat, then at its breaker if you can reach the panel on a dry floor. Southern California Edison is the electric utility the city lists, so call it or 911 if water is near a panel or the power is behaving strangely.

Stay out from under a bulging ceiling, because water above drywall can drop without warning. Put a bucket under a drip, move furniture, and call this line for the water and an HVAC technician for the system.

What the Crew Does

  1. Confirm the AC is off. The unit stays off so no more water arrives while we work.
  2. Extract. Extraction units and wet vacuums remove water from floors, carpet and the pan area.
  3. Find the wet spots. A moisture meter and thermal camera trace water through ceiling drywall, insulation and wall cavities.
  4. Release and open. A bulging ceiling can be drained in a controlled way. Wet insulation is removed and small openings let framing dry.
  5. Dry. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference materials. See structural drying for how that is monitored.
  6. Clean. Surfaces are cleaned, and antimicrobial is applied where the wetness or delay calls for it. Mold concerns follow separate S520 practice.

Wet walls and ceilings often need patching after drying, and the drywall water damage page covers that part.

What Changes the Cost

  • How long it dripped. Weeks of slow leaking wets more material than one overflow.
  • Attic or ceiling access. Water above drywall needs more opening and drying.
  • Insulation. Wet insulation usually has to be removed and replaced.
  • Rooms affected. A leak that traveled down a wall and into a lower room adds scope.
  • Equipment days. More wet area means more air movers, dehumidifiers and monitoring visits.

The HVAC repair and any replacement parts are a separate bill from the drying.

Insurance Fit

Coverage depends on the cause. A sudden overflow or pan failure may be treated as sudden and accidental. Water that dripped for months from a clogged line is often treated as gradual and may be excluded, and a maintenance issue can weigh against a claim. Photograph stains and the equipment. Keep receipts for HVAC service and drying. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Prevention

  • Change the filter on schedule so the coil keeps airflow.
  • Have the condensate line flushed at routine service.
  • Check that the secondary drain pan is dry.
  • Look at the ceiling under an attic unit after a long hot spell.

Duarte local note: summer load and permits
