Carpet water damage restoration in Duarte, CA means pulling water out of carpet and pad, then drying the floor beneath so the carpet can often stay. The line is answered 24/7 and a crew goes out when one is available. Speed matters because carpet holds water like a sponge.

Carpet is really three layers. The surface fiber, the pad under it and the subfloor beneath the pad all react to water differently, and the answer to "can it be saved" is different for each one. Most of the work is deciding that layer by layer instead of guessing.

What Water Does to Carpet

Carpet fiber itself shrugs off water. The trouble sits below. Pad soaks up water and holds it long after the surface feels dry, and the subfloor can stay wet under the pad for days. That hidden moisture is what causes odor, mildew and a floor that smells musty after a leak that looked minor.

Water also attacks the glue that holds the backing together. Long exposure can loosen the backing from the fiber, which shows up as bubbles, loose seams or a wavy surface. Once the backing separates, the carpet cannot be stretched back into shape.

First-hour safety steps

Stop the source first by closing the shut-off valve at the fixture or the main. Keep power off to outlets and cords sitting in or near wet carpet, and do not use a household vacuum or a plugged-in device on a wet floor.

If water is near the panel or you smell gas, call 911 or the utility before calling us. Lift small furniture off the carpet and put foil or blocks under heavy legs so stain does not transfer.

Save the Carpet or Replace It

The water category decides this more than anything else. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into categories by how contaminated it is. Clean water from a supply line can often be extracted and the carpet dried in place, while the pad is often removed and replaced because it is cheap to replace and slow to dry.

Water that has picked up contamination changes the answer. Carpet and pad exposed to sewage or flood water are normally removed rather than cleaned. We would rather tell you that plainly than save material that should go. Health questions belong with your physician.

Our Carpet Drying Process

  1. Test the water and the floor. A moisture meter and a thermal camera show how far the water traveled under the carpet.
  2. Extract. Extraction units pull water out of the fiber and pad before any drying equipment starts.
  3. Decide on the pad. Wet pad is usually cut and removed, and the carpet is lifted from the tack strip along the edges so air can reach both sides.
  4. Dry. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on the carpet and the exposed subfloor, with floor drying equipment where needed.
  5. Clean and treat. Dried carpet is cleaned and an antimicrobial is applied. Odor is dealt with at the source, not covered up.
  6. Verify. Readings are checked on each visit until the subfloor matches dry material, and then the carpet is reinstalled or replaced.

What Changes the Cost

The scope is explained before work starts. The factors below drive the job.

  • Water category. Clean water is simpler than contaminated water, which means removal and disposal.
  • Area and depth. More square footage and saturated pad mean more extraction and more equipment.
  • Time wet. Carpet that has sat for days is harder to save than carpet extracted the same day.
  • Carpet and pad type. Backing, seams and pad thickness change what can be dried in place.
  • Subfloor. Concrete, plywood and boards dry on different schedules.

Insurance Fit

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a failed supply line or a pipe that burst, is often covered. A gradual leak is often excluded, and flood damage is usually under a separate policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Photograph the carpet before removal and keep receipts for emergency steps.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting for the carpet to dry on its own. The pad usually stays wet.
  • Using a household fan and calling it done, which can leave the subfloor wet.
  • Pulling up carpet without knowing the water category.
  • Putting a rug or furniture back before the floor has been checked with a meter.

Duarte local note: renters and the 24 to 48 hour window

The CDC advises cleaning and drying wet items or removing them within 24 to 48 hours. In a wet carpet job that is the practical deadline, so call the same day rather than waiting to see whether it dries.
