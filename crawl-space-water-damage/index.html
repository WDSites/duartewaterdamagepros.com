Not every Duarte home has a crawl space, and we do not know what share do. If yours does, water down there is easy to miss and slow to dry.

How Water Gets Under a Duarte House

A crawl space is the shallow gap between the ground and the floor framing of a house with a raised foundation. Water gets in through plumbing leaks above, a failed drain line, runoff pushed against the foundation during heavy rain, or a water line breaking in the soil.

Southern California has a dry summer and a rainy winter, so crawl space water often appears after a storm rather than in the dry months.

The worry is what the water touches. Subfloor sheathing, floor joists, insulation and ductwork all sit within reach. Wet insulation drops away from the floor and holds water against the wood. Moisture also rises into the living space above, which is why a damp smell inside the house sometimes starts under it.

Symptoms That Point Under the House

Soft or bouncy floor

Subfloor panels that have been wet may swell and lose stiffness. Mark the spot and keep heavy items off it.

Musty smell with no visible leak

Damp air from below can travel up through gaps around pipes and vents. Ask for moisture readings before replacing anything.

Cold or wet floor in one room

Wet insulation and damp air chill the floor. This often sits above a plumbing run.

Standing water at the access hatch

Do not climb in. Photograph it from the opening and call.

Why We Treat Crawl Spaces Carefully

A crawl space is a confined job that is done on hands and knees. There are exposed wires, sharp nails and fasteners, pipe edges, and often loose insulation. Water makes every one of those worse. Standing water near wiring is an electrical risk, so power to the area may need to be shut off before anyone goes in.

That is why we ask you not to go under the house to look. Photograph the opening from outside and tell dispatch what you see. Crews work with lights, protective gear and a plan for getting equipment in and out.

First-hour safety steps

Do not enter the crawl space if there is standing water, a smell of gas or any exposed wiring. Shut off the water at the main if a pipe is the cause, and turn off power to that part of the house at the panel only if you can do it dry.

Call 911 for electrical danger or gas, a plumber for a broken line, and this 24/7 line for the water and the drying. If runoff is the source, do not wade through it to the hatch.

Our Process Under the Floor

  1. Access and hazard check. The crew locates the opening, checks for electrical and sharp hazards and decides how to stage equipment.
  2. Water removal. Submersible pumps and extraction units pull standing water off the ground and out of low pockets.
  3. Moisture mapping. A moisture meter and thermal camera show how far water has climbed into joists, subfloor and sill plates.
  4. Wet material removal. Saturated insulation that has dropped or soaked through is removed and bagged. A HEPA vacuum helps with dust and debris.
  5. Drying. Air movers push air along the framing and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out. Vapor barrier sheeting is checked and dried where possible.
  6. Monitoring. Readings are repeated until the wood reaches a dry standard under the published ANSI/IICRC S500 reference.

What Changes the Cost

  • Depth and volume of water. More standing water means longer pumping.
  • Access. A tight hatch or low clearance changes how equipment gets in and how many hands are needed.
  • Wet insulation and vapor barrier. These add removal and disposal.
  • Framing affected. Joists and subfloor that stayed wet take longer to dry.
  • Source. A continuing leak or inflow has to be stopped before drying can hold.

Insurance Fit

Sudden and accidental water, such as a supply line that burst under the house, is often covered. A slow leak that has run for months is often excluded, and flood or surface water from outside is usually covered only by separate flood insurance. Check your policy, ask your insurer, and keep photos and receipts. We do not promise approval or payout.

Mold and Moisture

EPA guidance says moisture control is the key to mold control, and a damp crawl space is a place where moisture lingers. Drying quickly matters. If the framing already looks stained or smells musty, see mold remediation. California does not have a state license for mold work or state exposure limits, per the Department of Public Health, so ask any company what standard it follows and request a written scope.

Duarte local note: runoff, flood maps and sandbags

LA County Public Works advises using sandbags to direct runoff, and lists sandbag locations on READY.LACounty.gov. Before a storm, think about where water will go along your foundation and whether it will be pushed toward your crawl space vents.

The City of Duarte FAQ points insurers to a flood map panel from 1999. Do not rely on that. Look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to see where you stand today.
