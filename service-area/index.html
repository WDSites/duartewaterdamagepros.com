The Foothill Setting

All five towns sit along the base of the San Gabriel Mountains, and that shapes the calls we get. Wildfire burn areas above these cities can send mud and debris downhill when heavy rain follows a fire. Each town page names the events that local sources document, including the Fish Fire areas above Duarte, the Colby Fire area above Glendora and Azusa and the debris flow in northern Arcadia.

Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow, according to the California Department of Insurance. If you live near a canyon, read your policy before the wet season and ask your insurer what is covered.

How to Tell Whether We Reach Your Address

The simplest test is the ZIP code. Duarte addresses in 91010 and 91008 are our home territory. Monrovia 91016, Azusa 91702, Arcadia 91006 and 91007, and Glendora 91740 and 91741 each have a dedicated page with local detail. If your ZIP is not on the list, call anyway, because the line is staffed at all hours and dispatch can tell you quickly.

Do not wait for the perfect description. Dispatch does not need a diagnosis, only what you can see. Tell them where the water started, how far it has spread, whether the floor around the electrical panel is wet and whether anything smells like sewage. Those four answers decide the safety steps and the equipment.

When you are comparing providers in any of these towns, ask what the written scope will cover and whether drying and repair are priced separately. The about page lists other questions worth asking.

Every Area Gets the Same Basics

Every town gets the same dispatch line, the same written scope before work starts and the same documentation for your insurer. Local details change by town, such as who runs the water, who issues permits and how foothill storms behave. Our town pages cover those details so you know who to call besides us. For the services themselves, start at our services overview.

One more practical tip applies in every town. Before you call, take a quick set of photos from the doorway of the affected room, because the first photos show how the water looked before anything moved. Then call, and let dispatch walk you through the safe steps. Photos taken after cleanup begins show far less, and they are the pictures your adjuster will ask for first. Save them to your phone, and back them up if you can.
