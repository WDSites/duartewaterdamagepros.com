Emergency flood damage restoration in Duarte, CA is for water that came from outside the building, whether storm runoff, a street that overflowed, mud from a hillside or groundwater rising through a floor. This is different from a leak, because flood water usually carries silt and bacteria and is handled as contaminated. Dispatch is answered 24/7 during storms, and a crew is sent as soon as one is available.

If water is still rising, leave low areas and call 911 for any danger. Do not wade through moving water, do not touch electrical equipment while standing in water, and do not drive through flooded roads. Restoration begins once the area is safe.

When Flooding Is an Emergency

Flood water is an emergency from the first inch, because it brings silt, debris and bacteria into the house and soaks into flooring and walls quickly. Mud dries into a crust that is harder to remove, and carpet and pad that sat in flood water are almost always discarded. A rainstorm that sends water into a garage, ground floor or crawl space calls for fast extraction.

Staying safe around flood water

Treat flood water as contaminated. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter, keep children and pets away, and do not run electrical equipment in a wet area. Call 911 for any danger to people, and call the utility or 911 about electrical or gas risk before calling the restoration line.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

Basements and crawl spaces take on water from several directions. Storm runoff can come in at window wells and doors, groundwater can push through the floor and walls, and a sewer line can back up through a floor drain. Each source changes what the crew does. Surface runoff is extracted and the room is dried. Groundwater seepage needs a cause fixed by another trade, and sewage needs protective gear.

The sequence is extraction first with pumps, then removal of anything porous that cannot be dried, then cleaning and sanitizing, then structural drying with air movers and dehumidifiers. Concrete is slow to dry and holds moisture for days, so monitoring continues until readings are acceptable. Stored boxes, furniture and electronics are triaged in the same visit.

Restoration is not waterproofing. If a basement floods whenever it rains, the cause may be grading, a drain, a window well or a foundation crack, and those are repaired by other contractors. We dry and clean what flooded and tell you plainly when a different trade is needed.

Sewage-Contaminated Flood Water

When storm drains and sewers are overwhelmed, floodwater can carry sewage into a house, and this is classified as the most contaminated category in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. The category decides the work. Everything porous that touched it, including carpet, pad, lower drywall and insulation, comes out. Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated, and the structure is dried before any rebuild.

Crews use protective gear, HEPA air filtration during demolition and sealed disposal of contaminated materials. Do not try to dry contaminated carpet yourself, because drying does not make it safe again. Photograph everything before removal so your insurance file is complete.

What Affects the Cost of Flood Restoration

  • Water category. Flood water is treated as contaminated, which raises removal and disposal needs.
  • Depth and area. Deeper water soaks higher into walls and more square footage needs more equipment.
  • Mud and debris. Silt must be shoveled and washed out before drying starts.
  • Materials. Carpet, pad, insulation and lower drywall are usually replaced rather than dried.
  • Drying days. Concrete and framing dry slowly, so monitoring continues until readings reach the dry standard.

Insurance Note

The California Department of Insurance states that standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage, and that policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. FEMA notes that a flood policy typically takes 30 days to become active, so it will not help for a storm that is already on the way. Read your policy and ask your insurer what applies to your loss.

Duarte local note: foothill runoff and burn areas

Duarte lies below canyons that have burned. USGS documented a debris flow in a small canyon above the Las Lomas debris basin in Duarte on January 20, 2017, after the June 2016 Fish Fire. On February 17, 2017, news reports described mud overtopping K-rails on Mel Canyon Road and evacuation orders for 202 homes. If you live near the canyons, ask the county where free sandbags are available and check your policy for mudflow wording.

A June 2022 Fish Fire burned about 39 acres, and the LA County Public Works brief named Melcanyon Road and Deerlane Drive, with city K-rails limiting impacts to access for 44 properties. The City of Duarte FAQ also points insurers to an old 1999 flood map panel, so check your address on the current FEMA Flood Map Service Center rather than relying on it.
