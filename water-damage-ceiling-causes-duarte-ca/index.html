Ceiling Water Damage Causes in Duarte, CA

A ceiling stain only shows where water finally gave up, not where it started. Here is how to read the clues and what to do before the drywall fails.

By the Duarte Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026 · Updated October 27, 2026

☎ (626) 879-0276

Ceiling water damage in Duarte, CA starts with a stain, but the stain is only the last place the water showed itself. The wet spot you can see may sit several feet from the leak, because water runs along joists, pipes and the top of the drywall before it finds a low point and soaks through.

Painting over the stain without finding the source just hides a problem that will come back.

If the ceiling is bulging or dripping right now, read the safety box first and then use the table.

The four usual sources of a ceiling leak

Roof leaks

A roof leak tends to show up after rain. The stain grows during or just after a storm and may stop growing when the weather dries out.

Look for stains near an exterior wall, a chimney, a skylight, a vent pipe or a roof valley. The attic insulation above the stain is often soaked, and the roof decking may show dark streaks or a nail hole with daylight through it.

Supply and drain lines

Pressurized supply lines leak in any weather. The stain keeps growing, may feel cool and damp, and often sits under a bathroom, kitchen or laundry. Drain lines leak only while water is running, so a stain may appear after a shower or a dishwasher cycle.

Try a quick test. Turn off every fixture, then watch the dial on your water meter. If it still moves, a supply line is leaking somewhere. If it holds still, suspect a drain, the roof or a condensate line.

Air conditioner condensate lines

An air handler in the attic or a closet pulls gallons of water out of the air on a hot day and sends it out through a small PVC drain line. When that line clogs, the drain pan overflows and the ceiling below soaks through.

The clues are clean water, no link to rain, a stain under or along the equipment, and a pattern that follows the thermostat. Switch the system off at the thermostat first.

Upstairs fixtures

A tub, shower pan, toilet seal or washing machine hose on the floor above can drip slowly for months. The stain appears directly below the fixture, often yellow-brown with a darker rim, and the drywall may feel soft.

Failed tub caulk, a weeping toilet wax ring and a cracked shower pan are the usual suspects. In a single-story home the same pattern comes from attic plumbing or a pipe where it passes through the roof.

What the stain itself is telling you

  • Brown or tan with a darker ring. The area has dried and been wet again, so the leak is repeating and is probably still active.
  • Round and spreading. Usually a point source directly above, such as a fixture, a pipe joint or a single roof penetration.
  • Gray or black spotting. The area has stayed damp for some time. Do not scrub or sand it. Mold needs moisture to grow, and any health question belongs with a physician.
  • Sagging or bulging. Water is pooling on the back of the drywall. The board is now heavy and can fall.

If the ceiling is bulging or dripping

Keep people and pets out from under it. If water is near a light fixture, ceiling fan or outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker panel and do not touch wet electrical parts. Let a crew release trapped water with the power off. If you smell burning or see sparks, call 911.

Triage for the first 24 to 48 hours

The EPA and CDC both advise drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to keep mold from starting. That clock begins when the material got wet, not when you noticed the stain, so move quickly even for a small spot.

  1. Stop the source. Close the main water valve, switch off the AC, or cover the roof opening.
  2. Protect people and power. Keep out from under sagging drywall and shut off the circuit if water reaches a fixture.
  3. Photograph the stain from three angles with a date, and mark its edge in pencil so you can see if it grows.
  4. Move furniture and rugs, then set a bucket and plastic sheeting under any drip.
  5. Run a fan across the ceiling and a dehumidifier if you have one. Do not seal wet insulation behind new material.
  6. Get moisture readings. A pin or pinless moisture meter, plus a thermal camera, shows wet drywall extending past the visible stain.

Which kind of help matches the cause

Once the source is stopped, the wet building materials still need drying. Professional water mitigation uses air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and moisture mapping to find and dry wet drywall, framing and insulation instead of guessing from the stain. Duarte local note: whose leak is it?

California American Water runs the Duarte water system, and its 2023 water quality report lists the source as 100% groundwater from the Main San Gabriel Basin.

For a ceiling stain, the practical point is the meter. Everything after the meter is yours and everything before it belongs to the utility, so if you see water surfacing in the street or parkway, the City of Duarte FAQ lists Cal Am's line for street leaks at 888-237-1333.

Sewer service works the same way. The City of Duarte contracts with the LA County Sewer Maintenance Division, which runs a 24-hour line at 1-800-675-4357 for spills and odor. A stain from a house drain is your plumbing. If several drains back up at once or you smell sewage outside, make that call.
